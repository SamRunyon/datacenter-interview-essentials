Power Path Essentials

The Power Chain (memorize this order)

Utility feed → Medium/low voltage switchgear & transformers → ATS (Automatic Transfer Switch) → Generator (backup) → UPS (bridges the transfer) → Distribution boards / PDUs → Rack PDUs / power strips → Server power supplies (dual-corded when possible).

Redundancy:

  • N — Exactly the capacity needed. Single failure = outage.
  • N+1 — One spare. One failure or maintenance window is covered.
  • 2N — Fully mirrored systems. No shared components.

Strong answers name the stages, say what each does, and mention you confirm the redundant path before touching anything.

Cooling & Airflow

Key Concepts

CRAC (Computer Room Air Conditioner) — Has its own compressor/refrigeration cycle.

CRAH (Computer Room Air Handler) — No compressor; uses chilled water from a central plant + fans and coils.

Hot aisle / cold aisle — Racks arranged so intakes face the cold aisle, exhausts face the hot aisle. Containment (curtains, panels, pods) prevents mixing and hot spots.

PUE — Power Usage Effectiveness = Total facility power / IT equipment power. Lower is better. Good sites are in the 1.1–1.3 range depending on design and climate.

When a rack is hot but room temp looks normal: check blanking panels, cable management blocking airflow, local containment, and the specific CRAH/CRAC serving that row.

Safety First

Non-Negotiables

  • Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) — OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147. Isolate, lock, tag, verify zero energy before work.
  • NFPA 70E — Arc flash boundaries, PPE categories, approach boundaries.
  • Test before touch — Never assume a circuit is dead.
  • Escalation — Know your authorization limits. Call it early with clear data (rack ID, readings, time, what you’ve already checked).
  • ESD — Ground yourself when handling server components.

Interviewers love candidates who talk about safety without being prompted.
